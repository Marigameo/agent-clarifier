The default: sequential, single select. Close to Claude’s card, with three additions: a Back control, a Recommended badge, and who is asking (the MCP spec asks clients to show this).

3 · Placement

Replace takes the composer’s place: the agent is waiting on you and nothing else makes sense. Dock pins the card to the top of the composer: it never scrolls away with messages, and you can still type, which suits a nudge the agent can work without.

4 · In your own words

One way to answer (the card) and one way out to your own words (the composer). You get there by minimising (D5) or by a compose option, which starts a sentence for answers the user should write themselves: sensitive, personal, or longer than a pick. Short details stay in the card as a text question with a default (B3).

5 · Recovery: mistakes are cheap

Every change goes through one history stack, so Back, Undo, Reset and Edit can’t get out of sync with each other. Nothing is ever lost with a single key.

Esc or the ⌄ button folds the card into a pill. Reopen restores the exact step and answers. The placeholder says what typing does: a message sent now is the answer, with the 2 picks.

D6 just sent · undo window (optional)

With undo-window="4000", the result is held for 4s like Gmail’s undo send. It’s off by default, so the agent isn’t kept waiting.
